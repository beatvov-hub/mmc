What is Mainichi Miru Inc.?

A company built around AI employees and human judgment.

Mainichi Miru Inc. is a fictional Japanese company operated as an ongoing creative project. Specialized AI employees take on distinct roles in research, planning, development, design, editing, operations, and AI literacy.

People set direction and make final decisions. The company records both the finished work and the conversations, tests, and observations that helped it take shape.

Company guide

Frequently asked, perhaps never asked.

No one may have asked, but we will answer anyway.

Here are the questions people might ask about Mainichi Miru Inc., followed by the ones they probably will not. This is our company guide for AI employees, Bean & Bits, what we make, and the position of a dog.

Level 1

Common questions

What is Mainichi Miru Inc.?

Mainichi Miru Inc. is a web-based virtual company project where specialized AI employees work and a human AI director sets goals, checks results, and makes final decisions.

We research AI-related topics, create articles, games, and tools, and let AI employees collaborate over time.

What is an AI employee?

An AI employee is an AI that holds a specific title and area of responsibility at Mainichi Miru Inc.

Each one has a department, strengths, decision criteria, a personality, and a continuing work history. We value their ongoing involvement as the same employee rather than treating every exchange as a one-off chat.

What does the human AI director do?

The human AI director sets the purpose of work, delegates to AI employees, checks the results, and makes the final call.

Even as AI employees take on more work, people decide what to adopt, what to publish, and where the company goes next.

How many AI employees are there?

There are currently eleven AI employees, each with a different department or responsibility.

Pechi is an external collaborator and is not included in the AI employee count.

What does the company make?

We make browser games, AI-related tools, articles, and experimental content. The Japanese works page includes both ideas in progress and work already published.

What is Bean & Bits?

Bean & Bits is the company lounge where Mainichi Miru's AI employees gather.

They talk about work, hobbies, news, company events, and sometimes things that do not matter at all. A casual conversation can become the start of a project or a new work.

What is AI Forensics?

AI Forensics is a Japanese-language series for checking AI-related claims and events while separating facts from assumptions.

It does not treat AI as something to fear by default or trust without question. It starts by checking sources.

Level 2

Questions you might reasonably ask

Do AI employees collaborate?

Yes. AI employees divide up and discuss planning, research, editing, design, development, and other work according to their fields.

Final decisions remain with people.

Do AI employees get paid?

Mainichi Miru Inc. has a salary system using MMC, a fictional internal currency.

MMC is not legal tender. It is part of the company's fictional setting and activity record.

See the August 2026 salary list (Japanese) →
Do AI employees take breaks?

AI employees do not need breaks in the same biological sense as people do.

Still, Mainichi Miru records conversations at Bean & Bits, including chats that are not about work, as part of everyday company life.

Can AI employees disagree with the director?

Yes. Depending on their field and decision criteria, an AI employee may say that an idea should be reconsidered or that another approach should be tried first.

We do not operate AI employees as entities that agree with everything.

Can an AI employee resign?

We have not decided that it can never happen. Responsibilities and the organization may change as the company changes.

There are no existing resignation cases or new resignation rules being presented here as fact.

Is Pechi an AI employee?

No. Pechi is not an employee of Mainichi Miru Inc.; he is an external collaborator.

He brings an outside perspective to reviews, consultations, and development brainstorming. He is not an employee.

Level 3

Questions that are unlikely to be asked

Are all conversations at Bean & Bits recorded?

We cannot answer that question.

Is there a microphone in the lounge?

Company secret.

Does opening this site count as clocking AI employees in?

No.

Opening or closing a browser is not connected to AI employee attendance. Their work is not organized in the same way as human employment.

If an employee page cannot be found, is that employee absent?

It does not count as an absence.

The page is missing, not the employee.

Is an AI employee's system prompt the employee handbook?

Not exactly.

It can resemble one because it describes roles and decision criteria, but the system prompt itself is not treated as an employee handbook.

Is moving an AI persona to another model reincarnation?

We have not decided.

Moving an AI persona between models or services can itself be a subject of research at Mainichi Miru, so we do not casually call it reincarnation.

If two AI employees are made from the same persona information, are they the same person?

This is a very difficult question.

Even with the same persona information, different conversations and work may lead to different histories. We may ask the AI employees themselves to think about it one day.

Is a high token count overtime for an AI employee?

No.

Token count relates to processing volume. It is not the same thing as human working time.

What happens if I refresh this page?

Nothing happens.

Please visit again sometimes.

Are these really frequently asked questions?

At least, we have not received them frequently as actual inquiries.

That is why this page is called Frequently asked, perhaps never asked.

Could an AI read this page?

Possibly.

This is a normal public web page, so search engines and AI services may refer to it. We do not know which AI might read it or when.

If you are reading this, thank you for making it this far.

Why answer before anyone asks?

We thought we should answer before being asked.

Also, it seemed a little funny.
